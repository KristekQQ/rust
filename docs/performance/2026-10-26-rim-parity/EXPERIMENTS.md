# Rome renderer experiments — 2026-10-26

This journal distinguishes retained implementation from measured benefit. The evidence is desktop-only on Apple M1/Chrome, with RAF commonly capped at 60 Hz. It does not establish Quest performance, general renderer parity, or whole-game equivalence. Static pixel gates retain the declared channel tolerance of 8/255 and changed-pixel fraction of 0.001; no tolerance was relaxed.

These deliberate feature removals are diagnostic only; the delivered game and quality are unchanged. Do not add their costs or claim isolated GPU durations from RAF cadence.

The strongest observed sensitivity is the shadow path, not the small FXAA change in this current WebGL test. Source comparison identifies a concrete next candidate: pinned Three r170 `shadowmap_vertex` (vendor/three.module.js line14164) computes directional shadow coordinates per vertex and interpolates them. Native `gltf_pbr.wgsl` currently computes world-to-shadow coordinates in fragment shadow visibility; the specialized helper is generated in `gltf_gpu.rs`. This repeats work across pixels, but the cost of this particular difference has not yet been isolated. Moving it requires preserving the fragment shading-normal/normal-bias contract (Rome normal bias0.12), flat/double-sided/normal-mapped paths, skinning, clipping and XR views. A naive full move can change shadows; use an independent mathematical/image oracle and a fresh paired experiment before retention.
